LOCAL RULES, NOT GLOBAL TARGETS

How local communication shapes behavior

Cell shape and exclusion use the official Artistoo Cellular Potts engine. Additional alignment, local attraction and contact-turning rules are phenomenological parameters inspired by collective systems; cells are not treated literally as birds or ants.

Optional mesenchymal shapes use dynamic local fronts. Protrusion number, length and direction change through extension, turning, lateral branching and retraction. A front extends further only when actual cytoplasm catches up. Sustained membrane contacts stabilize connections, which break on separation or death. Protrusions consist of occupied grid sites and retain nuclear, minimum-width and solid-wall constraints. U87 morphology depends on culture conditions; this mode is not calibrated to a cell line and does not model microtube transport or calcium waves. Morphology model and evidence limits.

Morphology references: U87 morphology and substrates (Andrade Mier et al., 2023) · Brain tumor microtube networks (Osswald et al., 2015) · U87 node-link morphology (Awan et al., 2021). No images, videos or code were copied from these studies.

Three simplified signal fields undergo secretion, diffusion and decay. Cohesion primarily affects normal cells. Tumor and abnormal cells release damage cues; immune cells release recruitment signals after recognizing a target. Movement samples local gradients and actual contact neighbors without looking up the nearest tumor. Solid walls block both movement and signal diffusion.

Resting immune cells patrol with directional persistence, without unconditional alignment or strong adhesion to other immune cells. Their separate contact-separation response weakens under damage/recruitment gradients and pauses during antigen scanning or killing. This does not alter immune–target interface energies, deformation constraints or contact killing. Immune daughters inherit activation and remaining divisions but require their own contact recognition to recruit others; existing recruitment memory decays.

Default normal cells are gray and abnormal cells brown. Tumors darken from gray to black as their inherited proliferation trait increases from 0.25 to 3 (gray level 88 to 0). Actual growth also depends on nutrients, stress and the global growth parameter. Resting immune cells are pale red; contact recognition turns them deep red until activation memory expires, including inherited memory in daughters. Chemotaxis or incomplete scanning alone does not change their color.

Nuclei are shown by default. Their blue, red or pale-gold contours come from the mechanical nuclear regions. Colors are independently editable. Low overlay opacity protects body colors; above 50% this protection gradually relaxes, reaching full overlay intensity at 100%. Outlines and nuclei are drawn last. Signal display uses a fixed nonlinear enhancement; hover readings report raw concentrations. Act is optional by cell type: red marks newly expanded active pixels, then yellow and green fade back to body color. This is a cytoskeletal polarity proxy, not measured F-actin concentration.

Manual insertion attempts local yielding in a draft grid. Bodies deform and nuclei move along continuous valid pixel paths. Section-area reduction is limited, preserving total target biomass. Changes commit only if all bodies remain connected, nuclei intact and walls respected; failure leaves the scene unchanged. This is tissue editing without advancing MCS, not a migration mechanism. Inserted cells occupy real grid sites and must accumulate recognition through new physical contacts.

Under crowding, visible target area gradually decreases within the yielding limit and recovers when pressure is released. Total target biomass is retained, including off-plane metabolic demand. Growth slows when actual area lags the section target; below 90%, new biomass pauses until space returns. This avoids growth raising hard area constraints indefinitely in packed tissue. It neither stops movement directly nor removes existing biomass. Bodies remain four-neighbor connected; short protrusions must widen before extending. Protected nuclei move one grid site at a time within their bodies. Narrow gaps and walls still block passage. Off-plane reserve has no separate position or hidden contacts.

Nuclear contours have stable individual variation and limited, gradual deformation with nearby body elongation or compression. Pausing freezes them. Contours remain inside the original protected nuclear disk, preserving collision and passage limits; this is not full nuclear-envelope mechanics. Reference: Constrained migration and nuclear deformation (Wolf et al., JCB 2013).

Custom scenes start empty and paused at 0 MCS. Place cells, signals and walls, then start. The cell eraser removes whole touched bodies and counts manual removals separately from biological deaths. Erasing an already dying cell preserves its trigger record and marks it manually interrupted. Randomize samples moderate model parameter ranges and a seeded mixture of normal, tumor and immune founders; it does not guarantee a particular biological outcome.

Oxygen, glucose and acid load depend on local occupied area, with diffusion, consumption, clearance and medium supply. Sustained hypoxia, nutrient shortage and acidity accumulate damage, reducing proliferation and immune motility/killing. Motile cells can escape along local resource gradients. These are normalized model quantities; acid load is not experimental pH and supply is not a vascular network.

Immune cells must sustain physical contact along a shared grid edge with an antigen-positive tumor or abnormal cell before recognition and damage delivery. Healthy normal cells are not attacked. Recognition enables temporary recruitment and limited clonal expansion. Chemotaxis alone does not activate recruitment. Killing stops upon separation; recruitment memory may briefly persist. Immune division capacity decreases along lineages; tumor generations are unlimited.

The inflammation-associated transformation control treats local damage and recruitment signals as an inflammation proxy. Sustained exposure increases stochastic normal → abnormal risk; 0 disables this pathway independently of metabolic stress. Further transformation still requires time in the abnormal state and a second random event; immune surveillance may intervene first. The onset preset uses 1; other presets use 0. References: NF-κB/IL-6 and transformation (Iliopoulos et al., 2009) and IL-6/STAT3 and inflammation-associated cancer (Grivennikov et al., 2009). This uncalibrated scenario hypothesis does not equate recruitment signals with IL-6 or imply that all inflammation causes cancer.

Normal cells can die from sustained metabolic stress. Transformation uses two stochastic stages. First, stress can create an abnormal cell with some antigen but normal movement/metabolism, no automatic expansion and no manual division. After at least 160 MCS, an independent event may activate tumor growth, invasion and inheritance. Position, body, total target mass, existing damage and contacts are preserved. Abnormal cells may be killed by immune cells, die from stress or remain abnormal.

Transformation probabilities are uncalibrated model assumptions, not real disease risks. Hypoxia/DNA-repair and precancer immune-surveillance studies motivate mechanisms without proving that all hypoxia, glucose shortage or acidity causes cancer. The single contact-killing immune type does not reproduce the division of labor among real immune populations.

Presets set founders, parameters and supply geometry without scheduling forced transformation, spread or clearance. Onset uses a central acid-clearance factor of 0.05, smoothly reaching 1 outside; nutrient supply scales as 0.65 + 0.35 times that factor. Both transformation-risk multipliers are 8 to shorten demonstration time. New tumors have baseline tolerance 2.04–2.76 but retain damage and may still die. Cold tumors begin with 19 tumor founders and 3 peripheral immune cells. Distal statistics track original tumor clones beyond radius 55 and divisions occurring there; this 2D spread proxy excludes vascular transport and cross-organ metastasis. Hot tumors begin with 37 central tumors and 18 immune cells. Clearance requires all tumor bodies to disappear and 150 more MCS of observation; it is not a claim of clinical cure. Cold/hot presets disable new stress-induced abnormality.

Tumor tolerance, proliferation, invasion and antigen expression are inherited and may mutate independently at division. The environment selects among variants without producing directed mutations. Initial antigen fraction affects new founders only. There is no global population budget: space, nutrition, stress, actual body area, protected nuclei and valid geometric division determine growth and division. Immune activation and lineage division limits still apply. Performance warnings do not change these rules.

Immune killing uses a distinctive lysis illustration: rounding, limited section-area expansion, an amber color transition, then rupture and clearance. Spontaneous apoptosis shrinks away; environmental death uses gray-brown withering. Cause and morphology are separate concepts. Real cytotoxic killing may cause apoptosis or, under some conditions, pyroptosis. No gasdermin pathway is modeled and amber is not an experimental stain. Reference: GSDME and cytotoxic lymphocyte-mediated pyroptosis (Zhang et al., Nature 2020). Rupture remnants are not living cells.

This is a 2D section proxy with simplified nuclei and area yielding. It is not the original 2.5D surface-crawling model or full 3D tumor infiltration, and it does not solve real 3D volume or nuclear-envelope mechanics. Signals and parameters are uncalibrated. Manual dragging pauses all model clocks and performs local geometric yielding; releasing resumes the previous playback state and neighbor areas recover mechanically.

The 35 MCS warmup is included in model time. The GPU renders the display; CPU WebAssembly preserves sequential deformation attempts. Optional WebGPU fields use the same reaction-diffusion equations; automatic benchmarking includes transfer/readback and selects the faster backend. Playback changes MCS per wall-clock second without skipping deformation, contact or metabolic steps.

Experiment imports restore validated parameters as a new paused run, not a checkpoint. Current exports do not contain full lattice, field and random-generator state. Video recording captures only the visible arena, including pan/zoom, at a target 30 FPS and up to 1920 × 1080. The browser chooses a supported WebM or MP4 codec. Recording does not change model speed. Language switching is saved locally and does not reset the simulation.
